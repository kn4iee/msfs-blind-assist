BLUEBIRD 757-200 HOTKEY REFERENCE

Application Navigation:
  Ctrl+1     Focus Sections List
  Ctrl+2     Focus Panels List
  Ctrl+3     Focus Status Display field of the current panel (refreshes it if empty)
  F5         Refresh the current panel's Status Display now (it also updates itself live)

Tip: Press the same bracket key again to cancel a mode you activated by accident.

The BlueBird 757-200 needs two things running besides the simulator:
  - BBS Nest, BlueBird's companion app. Without it the cockpit takes every
    switch but no system responds. The app says "BBS Nest is not running"
    once after connecting when it is missing; start it and reload the flight.
  - The MobiFlight WASM module. Every switch is set by replaying the
    cockpit's own click through it; without it each control says
    "unavailable" and nothing is sent.

The sections (Overhead, Glareshield, Forward panel, Pedestal, Other) carry
every regular cockpit switch, knob and button, named as on the aircraft;
the Status section is read only. Each panel is one panel of the real
cockpit, and each guard sits right beside its switch. A section's panels
follow BlueBird's preflight flow: the Overhead starts with Electrical, APU
and IRS, the Forward panel with Brakes, and the Pedestal with the Control
stand, Engines, TCAS and transponder and COM radios. Inside a panel the
controls you use most come first (Electrical starts with the Battery, its
guard and EXT PWR; the MCP panel with the two flight directors and A/T
Arm). The Other section has the P61 panel, Oxygen, Flight deck door,
Forward attendant panel, Windows and sun visors, and Seats and stowage.
A switch's combo moves back to where the switch really is if the
aircraft did not take the change, and the app says "<name> did not move".
For the Emergency Lights and the two Stab Trim Cutout switches the app
opens and closes the guard for you. Buttons say "pressed" when pressed,
except the ones whose result can be read: an MCP mode or CMD button says
its light about two seconds later ("CMD R on", "LNAV off"), a speedbrake
button says the lever ("Speedbrake armed"), and EXT PWR says "EXT PWR on"
or "EXT PWR off". The cockpit's test buttons are held for you, 3 seconds
as in the cockpit. The rudder, aileron and alternate stabilizer trim
buttons and the manual cabin altitude buttons each hold their switch for
one second (their names end in "1 s").
The Electrical panel's status display shows external power (on, available)
and ground power.
The Pressurization panel starts with a Landing Altitude field: type the
destination's elevation in feet and press Enter; the app reads it back
("Landing altitude 1030 feet"). The 757 has no cruise altitude selector:
the cruise level is on the CDU's PERF INIT page.
The Forward panel's Landing gear panel has the Gear Lever (UP, OFF, DOWN):
OFF is a position the simulator's gear keys cannot select.
The Pedestal's Control stand panel has the Parking Brake, the Flap Lever
(UP, 1, 5, 15, 20, 25, 30), Speedbrake Armed, Down and Up, TO/GA and A/T
Disconnect. Its Engines panel has the Ignition selector, then each
engine's start selector and fuel control switch, in start order: Ignition,
L ENG Start, L Fuel Control, R ENG Start, R Fuel Control. Only the fuel
control switches of the engine type the aircraft has (PW or RR) are
listed. Its status display shows N1, N2, EGT, fuel flow and EPR for each
engine (read only, never announced), so an engine start happens in one
panel: Ctrl+3 jumps to the values, which update live while you read them;
while you are on a switch they pause until F5 or Ctrl+3.
The TCAS and transponder panel starts with TCAS Mode, then
has a Squawk Code field: type the four digits and press Enter; the app
reads the transponder back and says "Squawk 4743". An empty box says "Type
a four-digit squawk first."; letters or a digit 8 or 9 say "Squawk must be
four digits, each 0 to 7."
The COM radios panel comes next. COM1 is VHF L, COM2 is VHF C and COM3 is
VHF R. Each radio has a standby frequency field and a transfer button:
type the frequency (121.5, 124.85) and press Enter, and the app says what
the radio took ("COM1 standby 121.500"); the transfer button says both
("COM1 active 121.500, standby 124.850"). The status display shows the six
frequencies. A frequency outside the band says "Frequency must be 118.000
to 136.990, on a 25 or 8.33 kilohertz channel."
The Status section's Status panel shows the APU state, master warning and
caution, the AC bus power sources, the APU speed, the cabin altitude, the
chocks (In place or Removed), the autoland status, and the flaps (lever
and surface angle), gear, speedbrake and parking brake.
The MCP panel's status display lists the button lights.

Spoken on change (each can be muted with Ctrl+M):
  - Flaps ("Flaps 5"), gear ("Gear in transit", "Gear down", "Gear up"),
    speedbrake ("Speedbrake armed", "Speedbrake up 50 percent") and
    parking brake ("Parking brake set").
  - The MCP button lights ("CMD L on", "VNAV off") and the MCP values once
    they stop changing ("MCP heading 090", "MCP altitude 12000", "MCP speed
    250", "MCP Mach 0.78", "MCP vertical speed minus 1500"), "A/T armed"
    and "A/T disarmed", "Autopilot disconnect" and "Autothrottle
    disconnect".
  - The flight mode annunciations, starting with "FMA:" ("FMA: Roll: LOC",
    "FMA: Pitch: VNAV PTH, Pitch armed: none"). The Ctrl+M row is "Flight
    Mode Annunciator".
  - The EICAS messages as they appear and go: "EICAS warning: L ENGINE
    FIRE", "EICAS caution: ...", "EICAS: ..." for an advisory, and "EICAS
    gone: ..."; after CANC "EICAS: 3 messages cancelled", after RECALL
    "EICAS recall: ..." and the messages it brought back, or "EICAS recall:
    no cancelled messages". The thrust reference ("Thrust reference: CLB")
    and an N1 or EGT changing color ("EICAS: L EGT amber"). The advisory
    FMC MESSAGE is not spoken: the CDU's message is (see the CDU section).
    Ctrl+M rows: "EICAS messages", "Thrust reference", "Engine gauge
    colors".
  - The lower EICAS changing page ("Lower EICAS: STATUS page") and its
    status messages while STATUS shows (Ctrl+M row "EICAS status
    messages"), and the EHSI's messages (row "EHSI messages").
  - The CDU's EXEC light ("EXEC light on" when a change waits for EXEC,
    "EXEC light off" once it is executed) and a message the FMC puts on
    the captain's CDU while the CDU window is closed ("CDU: TAKEOFF DATA
    UPLINK").
  - Every COM frequency ("COM1 active 124.850"), the squawk ("Squawk
    4743"), the TCAS mode ("TCAS TA/RA") and the captain's altimeter once
    the knob stops ("Altimeter: 1020, 30.12", "Altimeter standard").
  - External power ("EXT PWR on", "External power available") and ground
    power ("Ground power connected", "Ground power disconnected").
  - The autoland status, the altitude alert ("Altitude alert"), the
    configuration warning ("Configuration warning"), each IRS when it is
    aligned, the engine start valves closing ("Left start valve closed")
    and the doors opening and closing.
  - Every annunciator light ("<name> light on", "<name> light off"), and
    any switch moved by something other than your own pick in the app (a
    cockpit preset, BlueBird's own automation, a hardware panel, the
    mouse), as the switch's name and its new position. Many switches
    moving at once, as when a preset loads, are summed up in one line
    ("Panel state changed: 24 switches"), and a flood of lights likewise
    ("9 lights on, 3 off").
A change you make yourself in the app is not said twice. The flood of
changes while a flight loads is not spoken.
The 757 does not say "glideslope alive": BlueBird's ILS receiver is its
own, and the simulator's nav radio does not follow it.
The stabilizer trim is spoken while it moves, about once a second, in the
units BlueBird's trim indicator shows ("Trim 5.3", as on the 737s);
Shift+T in output mode turns it off and on.

OUTPUT MODE - Press right bracket to activate

MCP Readouts:
  Shift+H    Read MCP Heading
  Shift+S    Read MCP Speed (knots, or Mach in Mach mode)
  Shift+A    Read MCP Altitude
  Shift+V    Read MCP Vertical Speed
  Each reads the value the MCP window shows, never the simulator's own
  autopilot values (they disagree with BlueBird's MCP), and then names the
  MCP mode buttons that are lit: "Heading 090, LNAV", "Speed 250, SPD",
  "Altitude 12000, ALT HOLD". They are the button lights, so an armed mode
  whose button is lit is named too; HDG SEL has no light, so heading
  select names nothing. In Mach mode Shift+S reads the Mach ("MCP Mach
  0.78"); while VNAV blanks the speed window it says "MCP speed window
  blank. Speed Intervene opens it."

Altitude:

  A          Read Altitude MSL

Airspeed:

  T          Read TAS (True Airspeed)
  G          Read Ground Speed
  M          Read Mach Number

Heading:
  H          Read Magnetic Heading
  U          Read True Heading

Navigation:
  N          Read the nav radios as BlueBird has them: each VOR as the CDU
             shows it ("VOR L 116.90 auto, GSU.", "VOR R not tuned."), a
             hand-tuned VOR with its course ("VOR L 113.90 remote, course
             095."), then the ILS course and what the ILS control panel
             shows ("ILS course 095. ILS 110.50." or "ILS parked."). The
             VORs are read from the first officer's CDU's NAV RADIO page.
             If the ILS panel cannot be read, the app says the frequency
             it set itself, marked as a count ("ILS 110.50 by count.").
  Ctrl+W     Read the active waypoint as the EHSI shows it: name, distance
             and time ("KOLM, 37.5 NM, 1533.3z"). In VOR or ILS mode it
             says so ("KOLM, EHSI in VOR mode"); with the display dark,
             "EHSI waypoint unavailable: display dark".

             left 12740, right 12740, center 0")
  Shift+F    The same in kilograms

Weight:

  Shift+W    Read gross weight in kilograms

Flaps:
  L          Read the flap lever ("Flaps 5"). When the flaps are not at
             the lever's position yet it adds where they are: "Flaps 5,
             moving, 3 degrees", or "Flaps 1, surfaces at 0 degrees" when
             they stay there (no hydraulic pressure).

Flap Maneuver Speeds (calculated for your current weight):
  Shift+1    Flaps up maneuver speed
  Shift+2    Flaps 1 maneuver speed
  Shift+3    Flaps 5 maneuver speed
  Shift+4    Flaps 15 maneuver speed
  Shift+5    Flaps 20 maneuver speed
  Shift+6    VREF 30 and VREF 25
  Each says the speed and "calculated" ("Flaps 5 maneuver speed 164 knots,
  calculated", "VREF 30 124 knots, VREF 25 126 knots, calculated"). They
  come from BlueBird's own VREF at your gross weight. Below 140,000 or
  above 250,000 pounds they say "Flap speeds unavailable."

Landing Gear:

  Both read the first officer's CDU and leave it on PROGRESS page 1. When

Displays (no AI key needed):
  Alt+E      Open the Upper EICAS window: a live list of the engine
             indications (EPR, N1, EGT), the thrust reference and every
             message, then the buttons Cancel (Alt+C), Recall (Alt+R) and
             Max Ind (Alt+M). A button says "Cancel pressed", and the
             display's own change follows ("EICAS: 3 messages cancelled").
  Alt+S      Open the Lower EICAS window: a live list of the page the
             screen shows and its values (the engine page: N2, fuel flow,
             oil pressure, temperature and quantity, vibration; STATUS:
             hydraulic quantity and pressure, APU, oxygen pressure, the
             control positions in words, the status messages and the brake
             temperatures by wheel), then the buttons Engine and Status,
             which press the two display keys; the page change is spoken
             ("Lower EICAS: STATUS page").
  Alt+N      Open the EHSI window: the Captain's EHSI as a live list (in
             MAP the route, the active waypoint and the VNAV points with
             distances and bearings, the wind, TCAS traffic and what the
             map switches show; in VOR and ILS the course, deviation and
             bearing pointers), then the Captain's EFIS controls, which
             show the cockpit's state and set it:
               Mode (Alt+M) and Range (Alt+R), two choice lists
               CTR (Alt+C) and TFC (Alt+T), buttons
               WXR (Alt+W), TERR (Alt+E), NAV AID (Alt+N), APRT (Alt+A),
               DATA (Alt+D) and WPT (Alt+P), check boxes
  In each window Tab moves from the list to the controls, the list
  rewrites only the rows that change, and Escape closes it. If a display
  cannot be read from the aircraft, its key describes it with AI instead
  (below).

AI Display Reading (needs an AI provider configured in Settings > AI tab):
  Alt+P      Describe the ADI (attitude and flight mode annunciations) and
             the AUTOLAND STATUS annunciator beside it (LAND 3, LAND 2,
             NO LAND 3, NO AUTOLAND) when it shows one
  Alt+I      Describe the standby instruments
  Alt+D      Describe outside scene
  Alt+E, Alt+S and Alt+N describe their display this way only when it
  cannot be read from the aircraft. The app moves the simulator camera to
  the display it is reading and puts your own view back afterwards; from
  an outside or drone view it says "Switch to a cockpit view first".
  The display is read as it presents itself: abbreviations such as EPR, N1
  and EGT and the units are not changed.
  The flight mode annunciations need no AI: they are spoken as they change
  and listed at the top of the 757 Autopilot window (input mode, Ctrl+P).

MCP Value Windows:
  Ctrl+H     Set MCP Heading (0-360, 360 is north)
  Ctrl+S     Set MCP Speed: knots (100 to 399) or a Mach number (0.40 to
             0.86, typed as 0.78, .78 or M.78). When you type a Mach while
             the MCP shows knots, or knots while it shows Mach, the app
             presses IAS/MACH for you first; when the aircraft does not
             take that (on the ground, for one) it says "IAS/MACH
             changeover is not available now". While VNAV blanks the MCP's
             speed window the box is closed: press Speed Intervene to open
             the window. Anything else says "Enter a speed from 100 to 399
             knots, or a Mach number from 0.40 to 0.86."
  Ctrl+A     Set MCP Altitude (100 to 49000, rounded to 100 feet)
  Ctrl+V     Set MCP Vertical Speed (-8000 to 6000, rounded to 100)
  Each window stays open for the next value; Escape closes it. Press Enter
  to set. About a second later the app reads the value back from the MCP
  ("Heading 090", "Speed 250", "Mach 0.78", "Altitude 12000", "Vertical
  speed minus 1500"), so you hear what the MCP took, not what you typed. A
  speed is set by turning the MCP's speed knob for you in quick bursts,
  allowing for the way BlueBird's knob speeds up when turned fast; it
  takes about one to two seconds, and the app checks and corrects it
  before it speaks.
  Each window also has the MCP buttons that go with its value, each showing
  its light ("LNAV: On"); Alt and the underlined letter presses one:
    Heading:           HDG SEL (Alt+S), HDG HOLD (Alt+H), LNAV (Alt+L)
    Speed:             Speed Intervene (Alt+I), IAS/MACH (Alt+M),
                       SPD (Alt+S), THR (Alt+T), FLCH (Alt+L)
    Altitude:          ALT HOLD (Alt+H), VNAV (Alt+V), FLCH (Alt+L)
    Vertical Speed:    V/S (Alt+V)
  A press is the same as on the MCP panel: about two seconds later the app
  says the light ("LNAV on"), or "HDG SEL pressed" for a button with no
  light (HDG SEL, IAS/MACH, Speed Intervene). Speed Intervene's button
  shows the speed window: "Window open" or "Window blank".
  There is no altitude intervene: on BlueBird's 757 the ALT SEL Push on
  the MCP panel does nothing. Enter a step climb on the CDU's CRZ page.

  Ctrl+B     Altimeter and minimums window:
             Altimeter box: type inches (29.92) or hectopascals (1013) and
             press Enter. All three altimeters are set together; a few
             seconds later the app reads them back ("Altimeter: 1018,
             30.05", "Altimeter standard"). Typing 29.92 or 1013 sets
             standard, the same as the Standard button.
             Standard, 29.92 button: sets all three to standard.
             Radio minimums box: type the decision height in feet (0 to
             2500, rounded to 10) and press Enter. The app steps the
             minimums and reads back what the ADI shows ("DH 200").
             The Status box shows the values when the window opens and the
             last result. Escape closes the window.
  Ctrl+P     757 Autopilot window. It opens on a list of the flight mode
             annunciations, four rows ("A/T: SPD", "Roll: LNAV, armed
             LOC", "Pitch: VNAV PTH, armed G/S", "AFDS: CMD"). Tab moves
             on to the buttons, each showing its state ("CMD L: On") and
             each with its own Alt key, which your screen reader says with
             the button:
               CMD L (Alt+L), CMD C (Alt+C), CMD R (Alt+R)
               A/T Arm (Alt+A), Captain F/D (Alt+F), F/O F/D (Alt+O)
               THR (Alt+T), SPD (Alt+S)
               LNAV (Alt+N), VNAV (Alt+V), FLCH (Alt+H)
               HDG HOLD (Alt+D), ALT HOLD (Alt+E), V/S (Alt+Y)
               LOC (Alt+G), APP (Alt+P)
               Speed Intervene (Alt+I), A/T Disconnect (Alt+X),
               A/P Disconnect (Alt+B)
             A/T Arm shows whether the autothrottle is armed, as BlueBird
             holds it. Speed Intervene shows "Window open" or "Window
             blank". A/P Disconnect is the captain's yoke button: press it
             again to silence the warning. The MCP's disengage bar is on
             the MCP panel only.
             Each press is the cockpit's own click; the button then shows
             the new state. When the aircraft does not take a press, the
             app says so about two seconds later: "CMD L did not engage"
             (or "did not disengage"), and for A/T Arm "A/T Arm did not
             arm" (or "did not disarm"): press it again. Speed Intervene,
             A/T Disconnect and A/P Disconnect say "pressed". Escape
             closes the window.

Radios:
  Ctrl+N     757 Nav Radios window, for the ILS control panel on the aft
             pedestal. It has:
             Status box: what N reads, and the FMC's runway ILS for
             reference ("FMC runway ILS KATL 09L, 110.50, IHZK, front
             course 095"; it names the airport because, on the ground, the
             CDU shows the departure runway's ILS). It is spoken once,
             when it has been read.
             ILS course box (Alt+I): type the course, 0 to 360, and press
             Enter. The app reads it back ("ILS course 095").
             ILS frequency box (Alt+F): type the frequency, 108.10 to
             111.95 (110.5, or 110,5), and press Enter. The app turns the
             panel's knobs for you, one click at a time, and says what the
             panel shows afterwards: "ILS 110.50 set", "ILS already
             110.50" or "ILS shows 110.55, not 110.50".
             ILS park button (Alt+P): turns the panel to PARK ("ILS
             parked").
             Enter applies only the box you are in. Escape closes the
             window.
             To fill the Status box the app pages the first officer's CDU
             to APPROACH REF and leaves it on NAV RADIO.
             The VORs are not in this window: the FMC tunes them, and a
             particular station and its course are entered on the CDU's
             NAV RADIO page (Shift+M, Alt+N), as on the aircraft.

General:
  Escape     Exit active hotkey mode

CDU (Shift+M):
  Opens the accessible CDU window for both CDUs, read as text from the
  aircraft. The list reads like the other Boeings' CDU windows: "Title:"
  and the page name with its page number ("Title: ACT RTE 1 LEGS   1/7"),
  then for each line select key its label and its numbered line ("3:" is
  line 3), and "Scratchpad:" last. A screen with nothing on it says so
  ("Captain CDU screen blank"). The window's title names the side you are
  on. Ctrl+Tab switches between the Captain and First Officer CDU and says
  which one you are on; Ctrl+Shift+L goes straight to the captain's and
  Ctrl+Shift+R to the first officer's. The two CDUs are independent, as on
  the aircraft. The first officer's CDU shows once its screen changes: if
  its list shows it waiting, press a page key such as Alt+R.
  Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+6    Left line select keys 1-6
  Alt+1 to Alt+6      Right line select keys 1-6
  (F1-F6 / F7-F12 when the alternate LSK setting is on)
  Alt+I  INIT REF     Alt+R  RTE        Alt+D  DEP ARR
  Alt+T  ATC          Alt+V  VNAV       Alt+F  FIX
  Alt+G  LEGS         Alt+H  HOLD       Alt+O  FMC COMM
  Alt+P  PROG         Alt+M  MENU       Alt+N  NAV RAD
  PageUp / PageDown   PREV / NEXT page (also Alt+Up / Alt+Down)
  Ctrl+Enter or Alt+E EXEC
  Alt+S               Scratchpad box: type, then Enter sends it to the CDU
  Alt+C               Clear the whole scratchpad
  Alt+Home            Back to the CDU display
  Backspace / Delete  CLR / DEL (on the display, or in an empty scratchpad box)
  Escape              Close the window
  A new page says its title and puts you on line 1. When the page changes
  around you but stays the same page (an entry that turns ACT into MOD,
  for one), the new title is said and the cursor stays on your line.
  What you type in the scratchpad box goes in at about 0.4 seconds a
  character, and the scratchpad is then read back, so you hear what the
  CDU took ("KATL"). If the CDU missed a key the window says what is there
  instead: "Scratchpad shows KAT" or "Scratchpad is empty". Holding a key
  down presses it once. CLR deletes one character or clears a message.
  Typing "-" presses the +/- key once and "+" presses it twice.
  If the link to the aircraft drops while keys are still going in, the rest
  are not sent and the app says "CDU connection lost, keys not sent".
  The check box "Speak CDU messages when closed" is the Ctrl+M row "CDU
  Messages While Closed": with it on, a message the FMC puts on the
  captain's scratchpad is spoken while the window is closed ("CDU: TAKEOFF
  DATA UPLINK"). This works from the moment the 757 is the aircraft: the
  window does not have to be opened first.
  Flight data (SimBrief flight, route, winds, performance) comes in on the
  CDU's ACARS and FMC COMM pages: MENU, ACARS, INIT FLT.

757 EFB (Shift+T):
  With the simulator running, Shift+T opens BlueBird's tablet itself as a
  page you read with your screen reader, in a window titled "757 EFB".
  Every page is read with its section and every value with its label, and
  every control is named by the tablet's own words. You use the tablet as
  a sighted pilot does: press its buttons, switches, check boxes and
  sliders, choose from its drop-downs, and type into its text fields.
    - A press says what came of it only where your screen reader cannot:
      a button's new words, a two-choice setting's new choice ("EHSI up
      reference: TRK"), or that nothing happened ("<name>: no change on
      the tablet.", "<name> did not change."). The app scrolls the tablet
      to a control before pressing it.
    - A text field is an edit box: type, then press Enter. The app types
      it on the tablet's own keyboard and reads the field back. Leaving
      the box without Enter changes nothing.
    - BlueBird's notifications and questions are spoken while the window
      is open.
    - Home goes back to the tablet's home page.
    - F5 reads the tablet again. F6 says whether the tablet is connected
      and which page it shows.
    - Alt+F4 closes the window (Escape is left to your screen reader).
  If a page does not come up it says "The tablet did not show <page>. Try
  again, or press F5 to read the tablet again."; if the tablet cannot be
  reached, "The tablet cannot be reached, so nothing was tapped."
  What the app adds to the tablet:
    - On the Settings, AIRAC data page, while you are not signed in, a
      "Sign in to Navigraph" button. It reads out the Navigraph sign-in
      code, then opens the sign-in page in your browser (only ever a
      Navigraph address). Signed in already, it says "Already signed in
      to Navigraph".
    - After the AIRAC page's UPDATE the app watches BlueBird's nav data and
      says "Nav data cycle 2611 installed. Reload the flight to use it."
      Reload before you fly. If BlueBird removed the nav data instead, or
      the update did not finish, it says so.
    - After FETCH SIMBRIEF OFP (with BlueBird's automatic loading on),
      IMPORT FROM SIMBRIEF on the Fuel page or START BOARDING, the app
      watches the load and says once each, in the aircraft's weight
      units: "Fueling complete. Fuel 16340 pounds: left 8170, right 8170,
      center 0." and "Boarding complete. Passengers 21090 pounds. Cargo
      3990 pounds: forward 3990, aft 0." BlueBird fuels in a few minutes
      and boards at its realistic pace, which can take half an hour. If
      BlueBird has not started after three minutes it says so ("BlueBird
      has not started loading. Is BBS Nest running? Check the fuel with F
      and the weight with W."), and likewise if the load has not finished
      after an hour. When your SimBrief plan cannot be read, it says it
      will not report fueling and boarding. The tablet needs your SimBrief
      ID, on its Settings pages or in the settings window below.
  With the simulator closed, Shift+T opens "757 EFB settings" instead:
  BlueBird's tablet settings, read from its files and changed in them,
  which is possible only while no simulator runs. Choose a section, change
  its settings, and they apply the next time the 757 loads. The Status box
  at the top says what was read and saved. Escape closes the window.

First Officer (output mode, Shift+C):
  Opens the BlueBird 757 First Officer window: flows the First Officer
  carries out for you, and BlueBird's Normal Checklist, whose lines tick
  themselves from the cockpit.
  Flows: Power Up, Preflight, Before Start, Engine Start, After Start,
  Before Takeoff, After Takeoff, Descent, Before Landing, After Landing,
  Shutdown and Final Securing.
  Checklists: Before Start, After Start, Before Takeoff, After Takeoff,
  Descent and Approach, Before Landing, After Landing and Shutdown.
    - Preflight finishes with BlueBird's tests, shortest first, each saying
      what to listen for: both stall warnings (the stick shaker), CVR,
      takeoff config (the configuration warning), the wheel well, engine,
      and engine, APU and cargo fire tests (the fire bell), the captain's
      and first officer's oxygen (each held 10 seconds), TCAS, and ground
      proximity last. The lights the tests raise are announced while they
      run.
    - Before Start turns the electric hydraulic pumps on before it sets
      the parking brake. Without hydraulic pressure the brake is left
      alone: "Parking brake left released: no hydraulic pressure. Turn on
      the electric hydraulic pumps first."
    - Engine Start starts the right engine first: start selector GND,
      fuel control to RUN, light-off, starter released. A start with no
      light-off is aborted.
    - Before Takeoff ends with a cabin call for takeoff; Descent makes
      the cabin call for landing preparation and reminds you to set the
      ILS frequency and course (input mode, Ctrl+N); Before Landing
      reminds you of the missed approach altitude.
    - Final Securing switches the APU off, waits two minutes for it to
      cool down, then switches the battery off.
  What stays yours is said as a reminder: the MCP, the CDU and ACARS, the
  flaps, the trims, the landing altitude and the gear.
  With the matching First Officer settings on, it also acts by itself:
    - At the transition altitude: "Transition altitude. Altimeters
      standard."
    - Passing 10,000 feet: "Above ten thousand. Landing lights off." and
      "Below ten thousand. Landing lights on."
    - Auto-engage autopilot on climbout: at 400 feet it presses LNAV and
      VNAV if their lights are not on ("400 feet. LNAV and VNAV on.").
    - Auto-manage center fuel pumps: on during ground setup when the
      center tank has fuel ("Center fuel pumps on."), off when it runs
      dry ("Center tank low. Center fuel pumps off.").

Notes:
  - The aircraft loads in its own panel state (cold and dark) wherever you
    start; the app assumes no power or engines at load.
  - The 757 plays its own 80 knots, V1 and rotate calls and its altitude
    callouts. So on this aircraft Takeoff Assist (output mode, Ctrl+T)
    does not call "80 knots" or "100 knots"; its centerline tone and pitch
    calls work as on every other aircraft.
  - The flaps, speedbrake and gear also work with the simulator's own keys
    and controls; the app says where they ended up. The simulator's gear
    key can leave the gear lever at OFF: Shift+G says the lever.
  - The engine values on the Engines panel come from the simulator's
    engine variables: compare them with the upper EICAS (Alt+E) at your
    first engine start.
  - The thrust levers move with the simulator's throttle keys, a
    controller or a hardware throttle. For takeoff, check the A/T is armed
    (Ctrl+P: "A/T Arm: On"), hold the brakes, press THR and listen for
    "THR on" (the MCP panel or Ctrl+S; in Ctrl+P its caption reads "THR:
    On") before you release them. "THR off" or "THR did not engage":
    keep the brakes held and check A/T Arm.
  - The simulator's trim keys move the stabilizer trim, and the app says
    the setting while it moves: trim to the CDU's TAKEOFF REF figure
    before takeoff.
