# BlueBird 757 CDU window — rules in full

Each section is the complete text of one rule. Its one-line form, under the same ID, is in `.claude/rules/b757-cdu.md`, which Claude Code loads when it reads the 757's CDU window, its row and typing helpers or the tablet client; the shared 757 rules are in [b757.md](b757.md). Background: [docs/b757.md](../b757.md).

## B757-55

- While the 757 CDU window (`BBS757CduForm`, Shift+M) is hidden it keeps working for one thing: a message the FMC posts on the captain's scratchpad is spoken ("CDU: TAKEOFF DATA UPLINK", "CDU: INVALID ENTRY"). `OnVisibleChanged` points the tablet client at the captain's CDU (`CoherentBbs757TabletClient.ActiveCdu` 1), whichever CDU the window was left on; the hidden client goes on scraping it once a second (every 500 ms while the cockpit watch list of [B757-11] is set); and each new captain scratchpad goes through `Bbs757CduMessageWatch.OnScreen`, which says what `Bbs757CduRows.NewMessage` judges to be a message. The window is made without being shown when the 757 becomes the aircraft (`BBS757CduForm.StartHidden`, from `MainForm.BBS757.cs`), so the watch runs before the first Shift+M. Never stop that hidden scrape, and never speak a scratchpad change that falls inside the watch's settle window after the window's own keys.
- Why the scrape must go on. The message is the only notice a blind pilot gets of an uplink, a rejected entry or an FMC warning while working in another window; a sighted pilot sees the scratchpad and the MSG light. The owner's ruling for plan 09 (2026-10-28): CDU scratchpad messages are spoken while the window is closed, as the FBW MCDU window does, with the Ctrl+M row "CDU Messages While Closed" (`Bbs757CduRows.MessagesMonitorKey`, also the window's check box) to turn it off. The speech comes from the client's push, outside MainForm's mute wrap, so the window checks that key itself ([VAR-8]). While the window is shown its own scratchpad read-back speaks the scratchpad, messages included, so nothing is said twice.
- Why the settle window. A scratchpad that changes is a message only if the pilot's own typing does not explain it. `NewMessage` is silent for a first reading, a cleared scratchpad, the same text, text that grew from the old text (an entry being typed) or is the start of it (CLR took characters off), and while keys are pending. But the hidden client scrapes once a second, so the last key of an entry can go out between two scrapes: the first push that shows the entry then arrives with no key pending and no earlier push of the text growing, and would read as a message. So when the window queues keys for the captain's CDU it tells the watch how long they take (`KeysQueued`), and a change counts as their echo until `Bbs757CduMessageWatch.SettleMs` (2,000 ms, two scrapes) after them; a push that still sees keys pending inside that window extends it. The client has one key queue for both CDUs, and the first officer's keys (the CDU page service's reads, the window's own keys on that side) never touch the captain's scratchpad, so they neither hold a message back nor extend the window.
- The price, accepted: a message that happens to continue the text before it ("INVALID" then "INVALID ENTRY") reads as typing and is not said. A message muted in Ctrl+M is dropped, not kept for later. The text is remembered whether or not it is spoken.
- `Bbs757CduRowsTests` pins it (`NewMessage_speaks_a_message`, `NewMessage_is_silent_for_typing_clearing_and_a_first_reading`, `Message_spoken_once`, `Typing_is_not_a_message`, `Typing_that_ends_between_two_scrapes_is_not_a_message`). → [b757.md](../b757.md)

## B757-56

- `Bbs757CduMarkers.Mark` writes "X " in front of each inverse (highlighted) run of a CDU row, as the other Boeing CDU windows mark a selected option, and never moves anything after it. The marker takes the two blanks in front of the run's first character when they are free (after text, one blank more stays, so `<FMC   <ACT>` would read `<FMC X <ACT>`). When the gap is too narrow, the missing cells are inserted and paid back by the next gap that has a blank to spare; a gap always keeps one blank, so words never merge. A row is longer only when no gap after the run can pay. Markers are left off, the rightmost first, only when the row would pass 37 cells.
- Why. The window's rows are read on a braille display as much as heard. A CDU row is 24 columns with a left field and a right field, and a braille reader finds the right-hand field by its column: a marker that pushed every later character two cells right would move the right field on every row with a selection and nowhere else. The window puts three cells in front of each row (a label row's indent, a data row's "n: "), and a braille line holds 40 cells, so a marked row may be 37 and no more: past that the display would cut the row or wrap it.
- What marks a selection was measured, 2026-10-28, in cruise, on eleven pages of the first officer's CDU and the captain's (ACT RTE 1 LEGS, PROGRESS, APPROACH REF, INIT/REF INDEX, PERF INIT, both TAKEOFF REF pages, MENU, KJFK ARRIVALS, ACT ECON CRZ, and a modified LEGS page): BlueBird's CDU sent only the color green and never an inverse cell. A selection there is drawn as text (`<ACT>` on MENU, `MOD` in the title, `<ERASE`), which the window already reads; character size marks labels, units and the page counter, never a selection. So only an inverse run is marked, in case a page not read then uses one, and nothing is marked by color or size.
- `Bbs757CduMarkersTests` pins it (`Columns_kept`, `Row_is_longer_only_when_no_gap_can_pay`, `Marker_is_kept_while_the_row_fits_the_braille_line`, `Markers_that_would_pass_the_braille_line_are_left_off`, `Blank_inverse_unmarked`, `Rows_without_inverse_are_the_lines_unchanged`). → [b757.md](../b757.md)

## B757-57

- The 757 CDU window keeps the cursor on the pilot's row by the row's identity, never by its index in the list. `Bbs757CduRows.Build` gives every row a kind (title, label, value, scratchpad) and the line select key it belongs to (1 to 6); when the screen changes on the same page, `BBS757CduForm` calls `Bbs757CduRows.Restore`, which finds the row with the same kind and line in the new list; if that row is gone (a label that went blank), the next row of the same line, its value; else the same index, clamped. Only a new page (another page name or page number, `Bbs757CduTitle`) moves the cursor to line 1's data row and speaks the page.
- Why. A label row is listed only when it has text, so a constraint deleted or entered above the cursor removes or adds a row and shifts every index below it. And the title changes on the same page: an entry turns ACT RTE 1 LEGS into MOD RTE 1 LEGS, and inserting a waypoint turns 1/2 into 1/3. Before plan 08 (2026-10-27) the window, cloned from the HS787's, restored the cursor by index and jumped to line 1 on any title change: an entry that turned ACT into MOD threw the cursor to line 1, and a row coming or going above it left it on another line, so the pilot had to find the line again before the next entry. The MD-11 MCDU window has the same rule ([MD11-17]).
- A new title on the same page is still spoken (the owner's ruling of 2026-10-27: never make ACT turning into MOD silent), and the cursor stays; a cursor restore itself says nothing. The three VNAV pages share one identity (their titles change with the speed mode) and are told apart by page number.
- `Bbs757CduRowsTests` pins it (`Restore_follows_the_row_when_a_label_above_goes`, `Restore_follows_the_row_when_a_label_above_appears`, `Restore_on_a_label_that_goes_lands_on_its_value`, `Restore_keeps_the_title_and_the_scratchpad`, `Restore_with_nothing_to_go_on_keeps_the_index_clamped`). The HS787 FMC window still restores by index; that is its own issue. → [b757.md](../b757.md)

## B757-58

- An entry typed in the CDU window's scratchpad box goes out with each key held `Bbs757CduTyping.HoldMs` (100 ms) and the next key `Bbs757CduTyping.GapMs` (300 ms) after its release, 0.4 s a key. Every other key keeps the tablet client's standard 250 ms hold and 400 ms gap. Never shorten the gap on one quiet measurement.
- Why. Measured on the first officer's CDU, typing `KATL1100.5` (ten keys, with a repeated "1" and a repeated "0"), on APU power:
  - 2026-10-26, a quiet sim: every pair of hold 250, 150 or 100 ms with gap 400, 200, 100 or 50 ms typed the entry right 5 times of 5, down to 100 / 50. The measured holds and gaps both ran about 15 to 20 ms over what was asked. On that day alone the choice was 100 / 100.
  - 2026-10-27, a busier sim (another display pop-out open), through the tablet agent's own press as the app types: 100 / 100 typed it right only 6 times of 10, each miss losing a repeated digit ("KATL100.5", "KATL110.5", "KATL10.5"); 100 / 200 was right 15 of 15 and 100 / 300 10 of 10.
- So the fastest pair that never missed on either day is 100 / 200, and the app types one step slower, 100 / 300: "KATL" in about 1.6 s against 2.6 s before. A repeated character needs the key released long enough to count twice, and a busier sim needs longer; a quiet-day result says nothing about either. A proposal to shorten the gap measures on a busy sim, with an entry that repeats a character, at least 10 runs a pair, and keeps one step of margin over the fastest pair with no miss.
- The safety net is not a reason to go faster: after a typed entry the window reads the scratchpad back once and says it as it shows when the entry landed, or "Scratchpad shows KAT" / "Scratchpad is empty" when the CDU missed a key (`Bbs757CduTyping.ReadBack`, `Mismatch`). The client clamps any gap to 50 to 1,000 ms; 50 is a floor, not a safe gap. `Bbs757CduTypingTests` pins the keys and the read-back. → [b757.md](../b757.md)
