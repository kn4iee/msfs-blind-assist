# BlueBird 757 First Officer — rules in full

Each section is the complete text of one rule. Its one-line form, under the same ID, is in `.claude/rules/b757-first-officer.md`, which Claude Code loads when it reads the 757's First Officer code (`FirstOfficer/BBS757/`); the shared 757 rules are in [b757.md](b757.md) and the First Officer's shared rules in [first-officer.md](first-officer.md), [first-officer-automation.md](first-officer-automation.md) and [first-officer-boeing.md](first-officer-boeing.md), whose rule files load on that folder only where their globs reach it. Background: [docs/b757.md](../b757.md), [docs/first-officer.md](../first-officer.md).

All four rules come from the owner's First Officer requests of 2026-11-08 (plan 29) and its review; the owner flew the result and reported on 2026-11-13 that every in-sim and flight check passed.

## B757-60

- The 757 First Officer's "APU: OFF" checklist lines and flow steps (After Start's `AS_APU`, Final Securing's `SC_APU`) read the APU switch: the state field `FO_APU_OFF` is the APU selector (`B757_OVHD_APU_SWITCH`) reading 0, unread while the selector is. They never read `B757_APU_STATE` (0 off, 1 starting, 2 running, 3 shutting down).
- Why. BlueBird runs the APU on through a cool-down after the switch goes to OFF. A hand tick is judged by its line's state 10 s after its action ([FO-9]), and while the line read the APU's running state, the cool-down outlasted that grace: ticking "APU: OFF" switched the APU off, and the First Officer then un-ticked the line and said "Unable to complete" over a switch that had done what was asked. The owner's ruling of 2026-11-08: the line is the switch, as on the 737s.
- Whether the APU has STOPPED is still `B757_APU_STATE` 0, and it is read where that matters:
  - Final Securing waits `Bbs757FoFlowDefinitions.ApuCooldownSeconds` (120 s, "APU cool-down", the 737s' two minutes, [FOB-22]) only when the flow itself sent the switch-off (a step that found the switch already OFF skips the wait), then waits for the state to read 0 (`SC_APU_STOPPED`, which stops the flow if it never does).
  - The battery goes off only once the APU reads stopped: `Bbs757FoChecklistDefinitions.BatteryMustStay` keeps it on unless the aircraft is parked with the engines stopped ([FO-25]), `B757_APU_STATE` reads 0 and external power reads off; anything unread keeps it. The battery is never the way to stop the APU.
  - "APU: START" lines read `FO_APU_RUNNING` (state 2), and their action holds until the APU runs (measured 58 to 80 s).
- Not recorded: how long BlueBird's APU takes to stop after the two minutes. `Bbs757FoStateEvaluatorTests.Apu_off_is_the_selector_even_while_the_apu_cools_down`, `Bbs757FoChecklistStructureTests.Apu_off_lines_read_done_while_the_apu_cools_down`, `Bbs757FoFlowTests.Secure_cools_the_apu_down_two_minutes_after_switching_it_off` and `Secure_RemindsTheGpu_AndGatesTheBatteryOnTheApuAndExternalPower` pin it. → [b757.md](../b757.md)

## B757-61

- The 757 First Officer never sends the parking brake SET without brake pressure, and never turns a hydraulic pump on to get it. Every "Parking brake: SET" line and step (Before Start, Shutdown, and the Before Start Checklist's) is left alone while `Bbs757FoChecklistDefinitions.NoBrakePressure` holds: neither the left nor the right electric hydraulic pump (`B757_OVHD_L_ENG_ELEC_PUMPS_BUTTON`, `B757_OVHD_R_ENG_ELEC_PUMPS_BUTTON`) reads ON, and neither engine reads running. An unread pump or engine counts as no pressure. The First Officer then says `BrakeStaysText`: "Parking brake left released: no hydraulic pressure. Turn on the electric hydraulic pumps first."
- Why. The owner, 2026-11-08: the brake "doesn't seem to fully work unless the electric hydraulic pumps are on", as on the iFly. The one failure of the parking brake step in the app's logs (2026-11-07) was real, not a late read: the brake was sent with no hydraulic pressure and did not set. The left and right systems are the 757's alternate and normal brake systems; the center pumps feed no brakes, so they do not count.
- So the Before Start flow turns the electric hydraulic pumps on first (right, then center, then left, the manual's order) and sets the brake after them. Outside that flow the First Officer does not reach for a pump just to set the brake: a pump is the pilot's switch, and a tick of the brake line with the pumps off says why it left the brake alone, with nothing written.
- The parking brake itself is BlueBird's `L:B757_FC_PARK_BRAKE`, never the stock variable ([B757-1]), set through the lever's toggle and read back 1.5 s later; a brief 1, 0, 1 after a set lasts about 2 s (logged 2026-11-06 and 2026-11-07), inside the grace. `Bbs757FoChecklistStructureTests.Parking_brake_is_left_alone_without_brake_pressure` pins the rule, an unread row included. → [b757.md](../b757.md)

## B757-62

- The BlueBird 757's center fuel pump ON gate sits at `Bbs757FoActionExecutor.DispatchAsync`, the one place every First Officer write passes: flow steps and their tuples, checklist ticks and the automation. Before it dispatches, it asks the shared gate (`CenterPumpGate.ShouldSuppressCenterOn`, which lists the 757 map's `B757_OVHD_C_PUMPS_L_BUTTON` and `B757_OVHD_C_PUMPS_R_BUTTON` beside the PMDG and iFly keys): an ON for a center pump over a center tank at or below the automation's off threshold is done with nothing written, and logged. An OFF is never gated: a dry pump must always be switchable off. Never gate a shallower call. It is the same rule as [FOB-10] for the PMDG jets and [FOB-18] for the iFly; it is written here, and not in the Boeing First Officer rule file, because that file's globs do not reach `FirstOfficer/BBS757/`.
- The tank is in pounds: the state field `FO_CENTER_FUEL_LBS`, the center tank's gallons times BlueBird's own fuel density, or 6.7 lb/gal (`Bbs757FoStateEvaluator.FallbackPoundsPerGallon`, the loadsheet's figure) while the density reads nothing usable. So an unread density never leaves the tank unknown, which would keep the center pumps from ever arming. With the gallons unread it is NaN.
- An unread tank is not gated: `DispatchAsync` passes NaN, and the gate's comparison is false for NaN, so the ON goes out. That is the 757's exemption from [FOB-16], written down: the PMDG readers route a fuel quantity through `FuelSystemLogic.SafeRoundToInt` so that NaN reads 0, and the 757 does not. It is safe because the automation (`Bbs757FoAutoManager`, through the shared `CenterFuelPumpAutomation` policy) decides nothing until the tank and every pump switch read (`dataReady`), and the policy rejects NaN, as the iFly's does; and the flow's own center pump step is skipped while the tank is unread (`CenterPumpTarget_IsNull_WhileTheCenterTankIsUnread`).
- The automation ("Auto-manage center fuel pumps", opt-in): ON during ground setup with the center tank above 1,500 lb and the wing pumps on; OFF in any phase once it reads below 1,000 lb for 2 s, "Center tank low. Center fuel pumps off."
- `Bbs757FoExecutorTests` (`A_center_pump_on_over_an_empty_tank_is_suppressed_but_off_never_is`), `Bbs757FoStateEvaluatorTests` (`Center_fuel_pounds_fall_back_to_6_7_per_gallon`, `Center_pumps_for_start_follow_the_pound_threshold`) and `Bbs757FoAutoManagerTests` (`Center_pumps_turn_off_when_the_tank_runs_dry_and_say_so`, `Center_pumps_are_never_touched_on_unread_switches`) pin it. → [b757.md](../b757.md)

## B757-63

- The 757's push of LNAV and VNAV at 400 ft AGL ("Auto-engage autopilot on climbout", `Bbs757FoAutoManager`) says only what a press did. `Bbs757FoActionExecutor.PushMcpAsync` answers one of three `McpPush` values for each mode: `NotPressed` when the light, read fresh just before, was lit or could not be read, or when the press could not be sent; `PressedLit` when the press went out and the light came on within `McpLightWaitMs` (3 s, read every 500 ms); `PressedNotLit` when it went out and the light did not. `Bbs757FoAutoManager.PushWords` builds the one line from the presses alone: "400 feet. LNAV and VNAV on.", "400 feet. LNAV on. VNAV did not come on.", and nothing at all when neither was pressed.
- Why. The MCP buttons toggle. The manager decides to push from the lights as the 1 Hz batch last delivered them, and the executor then reads each light fresh before it presses; between the two the pilot, or BlueBird, may have lit the mode. The first cut reported such a mode as a failure: it said "LNAV did not come on" when it had pressed nothing, because the fresh read had found the light lit or unread. A pilot who hears that presses LNAV, and toggles a lit mode off, in the climb. The whole-branch review of plan 29 found it (2026-11-08) and it was fixed before the owner's flights. So a light found lit or unread is neither pressed nor reported, and "did not come on" is said only of a press that went out.
- The words say "on", never "engaged": a lit LNAV or VNAV light is armed or engaged, and the app does not know which.
- The push itself follows the 737s' rules, which the 757's manager shares through `LnavVnavPushHeight`: the height is fixed at 400 ft AGL and each push is guarded by its light ([FO-16]), it fires only on a climb the manager watched from the ground, and the one-shot is spent only by a push or by both lights read ([FOB-21]). `Bbs757FoAutoManagerTests` pins it (`Climbing_through_400_feet_pushes_the_unlit_modes`, `Nothing_is_pushed_below_400_feet_or_descending`, `A_window_opened_above_400_feet_never_pushes`, and the tests of `PushWords`). → [b757.md](../b757.md)
