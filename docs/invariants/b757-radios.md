# BlueBird 757 radios — rules in full

Each section is the complete text of one rule. Its one-line form, under the same ID, is in `.claude/rules/b757-radios.md`, which Claude Code loads when it reads the 757's radio code; the shared 757 rules are in [b757.md](b757.md). Background: [docs/b757.md](../b757.md).

How the measurements below were made, unless a section says otherwise: MSFS 2024, BlueBird 757-200, BBS Nest running, MSFS Blind Assist closed; writes through the MobiFlight calculator path with a unique prefix, as the app writes; reads through SimConnect and through the Coherent debugger in BlueBird's tablet view. Every value changed was put back.

## B757-5

- Never read, register or announce from the stock NAV radio variables on the 757: `NAV ACTIVE FREQUENCY`, `NAV STANDBY FREQUENCY`, `NAV OBS`, the CDI and glideslope variables, `NAV HAS LOCALIZER`. `BBS757Definition.UsesStockNavReceivers` is false, which removes the shared "glideslope alive" and "glideslope lost" call-out and its Ctrl+M row for the 757 (`BaseAircraftDefinition`), and the 757's output N and input Ctrl+N are its own (`BBS757Definition.Radios.cs`, `Bbs757NavRad`), never the shared stock-NAV readout and window.
- Why. BlueBird's VOR and ILS receivers are its own and leave the stock variables alone. Measured 2026-10-25 at a gate: the VOR/DME panel showed 116.90 AUTO while `NAV ACTIVE FREQUENCY:1`, `:2` and `:3` all read 110.50 and `NAV OBS:1` to `:3` read 0, and nothing done to BlueBird's radios changed any of them: a step of the ILS panel's whole-megahertz knob was taken and left every stock NAV variable alone, and `L:BBS_ILS_COURSE` written to 95 held while `NAV OBS` stayed 0. Measured again 2026-10-26: with the ILS panel tuned to 108.70, `NAV ACTIVE FREQUENCY:1` to `:4` stayed 110.50 and `NAV HAS LOCALIZER:1` to `:3` 0. And 2026-11-05, in cruise: 110.50 throughout while BlueBird's own variables followed every click. A readout built on the stock variables says 110.50 forever, and the shared glideslope call-out spoke of a glideslope the aircraft was not using.
- Where the 757's values come from instead:
  - VOR L and VOR R: the CDU's NAV RADIO page, read on the first officer's CDU through the CDU page service (`Bbs757NavRad.Parse`): the frequency, the tuning mode letter (A auto, tuned by the FMC; R tuned from the CDU; M manual on the VOR/DME panel) and the station's ident. A VOR is tuned, and its course set, on the CDU, as on the aircraft; the app has no VOR frequency box.
  - When that page cannot be read (the CDU unreachable), BlueBird's own variables: `L:BBS_757_VOR_L_FREQ` and `L:BBS_757_VOR_R_FREQ`, with `L:B757_MCP_VOR1_MODE_AUTO` and `L:B757_MCP_VOR2_MODE_AUTO` (1 automatic, 0 manual) for the mode (`Bbs757NavRad.TuningFromVariables`); the ident is only on the page. Measured 2026-11-05 on BlueBird's update of 2026-10-31, which added the frequency variables (they are not in BlueBird's public table): 114.5 and 115.1 with both VORs auto-tuned, then 116.05 and 116.4 about 40 minutes later as the FMC retuned. Not measured: what a VOR's variable reads when it is not tuned.
  - The courses: `L:BBS_VOR_L_COURSE`, `L:BBS_VOR_R_COURSE` and `L:BBS_ILS_COURSE`, whole degrees, which take a direct write. A VOR's course is spoken only while that VOR is hand-tuned: in AUTO the CDU blanks its CRS field and ignores an entry (measured).
  - The ILS frequency: [B757-10]. The FMC's runway ILS, for reference: the APPROACH REF page.
- `Bbs757GlideslopeGateTests` pins the gate (`Bbs757_registers_no_glideslope_row`, `Bbs757_never_speaks_glideslope_alive`, and that the PMDG 737 keeps both). → [b757.md](../b757.md)

## B757-8

- The 757's VHF L, C and R are the simulator's own COM 1, 2 and 3. `Bbs757Radios` and `BBS757Definition.Radios.cs` read the active and standby frequencies from the stock `COM ACTIVE FREQUENCY:n` and `COM STANDBY FREQUENCY:n`, set a typed standby with the stock event (`COM_STBY_RADIO_SET_HZ` for COM 1, which has no digit, `COM2_STBY_RADIO_SET_HZ`, `COM3_STBY_RADIO_SET_HZ`; the frequency in hertz), and transfer with the stock swap first (`COM_STBY_RADIO_SWAP`, `COM2_RADIO_SWAP`, `COM3_RADIO_SWAP`), a radio tuning panel's transfer button being the fallback for a swap BlueBird ignores.
- Why. BlueBird keeps no COM frequency of its own. Measured 2026-10-25: one step of `L:B757_VHF_L_FRAC_INC` moved `COM STANDBY FREQUENCY:1` from 120.900 to 120.905 (8.33 kHz channel names), `B757_VHF_C_*` tuned COM 2 and `B757_VHF_R_*` COM 3; `K:COM2_STBY_RADIO_SET_HZ` 118275000 set COM 2's standby, it stayed, and a VHF C knob step then continued from it (118.280). Measured 2026-10-26 at a gate: `127300000 (>K:COM3_STBY_RADIO_SET_HZ)` set COM 3's standby; all three stock swap events swapped their radio and the swap held (read 4.5 to 15 s later); and the transfer button of tuning panel 2 swapped COM 2 (VHF C), panel 3's COM 3 (VHF R).
- Never assume VHF C is COM 3. Before the 2026-10-26 measurement the belief was that panel 2 drove VHF R and panel 3 VHF C; it is the reverse: panel 1's selected-radio lights are `L:BBS_RTP_L_LIGHTS`, panel 2's `L:BBS_RTP_C_LIGHTS`, panel 3's `L:BBS_RTP_R_LIGHTS` (they read 2, 4 and 8 with VHF L, C and R selected), which `Bbs757Radios.RtpLights` holds in one place. A caveat from that measurement: every panel then selected the radio of its own number, so it could not tell "a panel's button swaps the radio its lights select" from "panel n always swaps COM n"; the fallback therefore reads the lights and presses the panel whose lights select the radio.
- MobiFlight drops a calculator string identical to the one before it: the second of two identical `(>K:COM_STBY_RADIO_SWAP)` calls did nothing (measured the same day), which is why every write goes out with a unique prefix ([SIM-17]). Frequencies are spoken and sent with a dot and three decimals whatever the PC's culture. `Bbs757RadiosTests` pins the parsing, the events and the transfer. → [b757.md](../b757.md)

## B757-10

- The 757's tuned ILS frequency is read from BlueBird's own variable, `L:BBS_757_ILS_FREQ`, through `Bbs757IlsVariableDisplay` (an `IBbs757IlsDisplay`): -1 while the ILS control panel is parked, the panel's frequency otherwise; the course beside it is `L:BBS_ILS_COURSE`. Both are read fresh, and `Bbs757IlsPanel.ReadingFrom` turns them into what the panel shows. Never from a picture of the panel. The owner's ruling of 2026-11-05.
- Why. Until BlueBird's update of 2026-10-31 no variable held the ILS frequency: the panel's knobs are step requests ([B757-59]), the FMC does not tune the ILS, and the panel, on the aft pedestal, is in no instrument view. So plan 04 read the panel's display from a capture of the simulator window, the gauge's picture decoded as digits, with a small black box laid over the window for each read. It needed the window's size and scale to fit, cost about 0.5 s a capture on a 3840 x 2160 simulator, failed at night against a dimmed panel until its filter was changed, and showed its box in a corner of the simulator window at each read. The update added `BBS_757_ILS_FREQ` (it is not in BlueBird's public table). Measured 2026-11-05 in cruise at FL320, each read about 1 s after the write before it: parked, it read -1; one step of the whole-megahertz knob out of PARK, 108.1; one step of the fraction knob, 108.15; the steps undone, -1 again. It follows each click at once and agrees with the click model. The picture reader, its decoder, its fixtures and the box were deleted.
- The app's click count is the fallback, never the source. When the variable cannot be read (an older BlueBird build, a read the sim does not answer), a set goes by the app's own count of the panel (`Bbs757IlsDial`: `Bbs757IlsDial.LoadState`, PARK remembering .10, as a flight loads it, then every click the app sent, replaced by every reading), and its words say it went by the count: "ILS 110.50 set by count: the ILS display could not be read." (`Bbs757IlsPanel.ByCount`), and output N says "ILS 110.50 by count." (`Bbs757IlsPanel.CountPhrase`). With no count either (the app started after the flight loaded, or the connection dropped), a set is refused: "ILS tuning unknown: the ILS display could not be read." A frequency is never claimed from the count while a read works, and never said without the words that mark it as a count: the pilot's own mouse or hardware moves the panel without the app knowing. Other start types than a flight loaded at a gate (on a runway, in the air, a saved flight) were not measured for `LoadState`.
- Not measured: whether MSFS 2020 installs carry the variable (BlueBird ships one package for both simulators). `Bbs757IlsVariableDisplayTests` (`The_reader_says_parked_for_minus_one`, `A_variable_that_is_absent_answers_null`) and `Bbs757IlsPanelTests` pin it. → [b757.md](../b757.md)

## B757-59

- Each click of an ILS control panel knob is one calculator write of its own (`Bbs757IlsPanel.StepCode`: `1 (>L:B757_ILS_FREQ_WHOLE_INC)` or `-1 (>L:B757_ILS_FREQ_FRAC_INC)`), sent through the unique-prefix calculator call, since a click repeats the same string and MobiFlight drops an identical repeat ([SIM-17]). `BBS757Definition.SetIlsAsync` paces them: before each click a fresh SimConnect read of the ILS course (the sim answers it in a frame after the last click went out), then `IlsStepGapMs` (250 ms) after the click before it, the first click of a set waiting for what is left of that gap since the last click of any set. A read the sim does not answer stops the set there, saying how far it got ("ILS unavailable after 3 of 5 clicks."). The count ([B757-10]) moves with each click as it is sent.
- Why. Measured 2026-10-26 on the ground, about 30 frames a second, the result read from the panel's gauge:
  - The knobs are step requests. BlueBird takes a request and sets the variable back to 0 within a frame: a request read non-zero in exactly one sample of an in-page poll and 0 in the next, 29, 33 and 35 ms apart (minimum, median, maximum over 13 writes).
  - One write is one step whatever its size: a request of 2 moved one channel (108.35 to 108.50). So steps cannot be batched into one write.
  - Two writes landing in the same frame count once. Six fraction steps sent 1 ms apart moved 1 channel; 20 ms apart, 4 of 6; 50 ms and 150 ms apart, 6 of 6. One frame of 192 ms was seen in a 110 s window, so a fixed gap of a frame or two can still put two clicks in one frame; hence a fresh read before each click, which follows the sim's frames, and then the gap.
  - Through SimConnect the request is never seen non-zero: the per-frame read showed 0 in all 8,485 frames across 77 writes. The app cannot confirm a step there, so it counts what it sent and checks the result by reading the panel ([B757-10]) after one more fresh read and gap.
- The knob model the plan is built on (`Bbs757IlsPanel.Step`, `PlanSteps`), measured the same day: the whole-megahertz knob is a ring of five, 108, 109, 110, 111, PARK, then 108 again for +1 and the reverse for -1, with no end stop, and it keeps the fraction; the fraction knob steps through the ILS channels only, .10 .15 .30 .35 .50 .55 .70 .75 .90 .95, from .95 to .10 with no carry into the megahertz; while parked the fraction knob does nothing and PARK remembers the fraction (111.75, PARK, then 108.75). `Bbs757IlsPanelTests` pins the model and the set. → [b757.md](../b757.md)
