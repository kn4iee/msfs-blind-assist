# BlueBird 757 MCP, readouts and announcements — rules in full

Each section is the complete text of one rule. Its one-line form, under the same ID, is in `.claude/rules/b757-readouts.md`, which Claude Code loads when it reads the 757's MCP, readout and announcement code; the shared 757 rules ([B757-3] for the MCP speed among them) are in [b757.md](b757.md). Background: [docs/b757.md](../b757.md).

## B757-9

- The 757's flap maneuver speeds (Shift+1 to Shift+5) and VREF (Shift+6) come from `Bbs757FlapSpeeds`: VREF 30 and VREF 25 from a table measured on BlueBird's own APPROACH REF page, linear between its points, and the maneuver speeds from the owner's schedule over VREF 30 (flaps up + 80 kt, 1 + 60, 5 + 40, 15 + 20, 20 + 20). A weight outside the table, or not read, says the flap speeds are unavailable. Never extrapolate past the table's ends, and never compute VREF from a generic formula or another 757's table.
- The table. Measured 2026-10-25 at a gate on the first officer's CDU: a trial GROSS WT typed into APPROACH REF's 1L, the three VREFs read, then DELETE and 1L to give the FMC back its own weight. In thousands of pounds, VREF 25 and VREF 30 in knots: 140: 111, 109. 150: 115, 113. 160: 119, 117. 170: 123, 121. 180: 127, 125. 190: 131, 129. 200: 135, 133. 210: 139, 137. 220: 142, 140. 230: 146, 144. 240: 150, 148. 250: 154, 152. The FMC's own 178,600 lb read VREF 25 126 and VREF 30 124, which the table gives between its points. Below 140,000 and above 250,000 lb the FMC refuses the weight, so nothing is known there.
- Why. No variable holds the flap speeds, and BlueBird's FMC computes its own VREF. The table is not a straight line (4 kt for every 10,000 lb, but 3 kt from 210,000 to 220,000), so a formula fitted to part of it is wrong elsewhere, and a speed spoken as a flap speed has to be the one the aircraft's own page would show. The schedule is the manual's ("approximately VREF 30 + 40" for flaps 5, "VREF 30 + 80" flaps up) and the owner's ruling that the standard flap table is good; every sentence ends "calculated", since the app worked it out.
- `Bbs757FlapSpeedsTests` pins the points, a weight between two, the speeds at 178,600 lb, the refusal outside the table and the sentences under de-DE. → [b757.md](../b757.md)

## B757-11

- The 757's cockpit-wide announcements (every annunciator light worth a pilot's attention, and every switch moved by something other than the pilot's own pick in the app) watch their L:vars through the tablet agent's watch list, never as SimConnect variables. `CoherentBbs757TabletClient.SetWatchList` gives the agent the names; the agent keeps each one's last value and answers a poll with only the changes; the client polls every `WatchIntervalMs` (500 ms) on the socket it already holds on BlueBird's tablet view and raises `LvarsChanged`; `Bbs757CockpitAnnouncements` turns the changes into speech.
- Why not SimConnect rows. The watch covers about 356 L:vars. Registered as variables they would more than fill what the definition can carry (the 757 keeps under 900 variables, and only a continuous, announced row rides the 1 Hz batch at all), and each would need its own slot in a data definition. The Coherent debugger reads all 2,173 of BlueBird's L:vars in one call in about 0.2 s (measured 2026-10-25), and the tablet view's one socket is already the app's ([DBG-9]: a view takes one debugger client), so the watch costs one call each half second and no SimConnect slot. The list survives an aircraft reload: the client sends it again to a reinstalled agent, and the first change set after that is a silent baseline.
- One owner for each spoken row. `Bbs757LightTable.WatchSet` builds the list: every light of `Bbs757LightTable.All`, plus the state var of every map control a pilot can leave in a position (not a momentary or held button, nor a control with no state var), less every name it is handed as announced elsewhere, each name once. `BBS757Definition.CockpitWatched` hands it the names of the definition's own announced rows that have a Ctrl+M row (a silent row, read only to phrase another's, speaks nothing and does not count) and `SpokenByOtherRows`: the flap lever, the gear lever, the parking brake and the A/T arm switch, whose moves another row speaks under a different variable. Without that, a light or switch a definition row already announces (the EXT PWR light, the IRS align lights, the TCAS mode knob, the autopilot disconnect light) would be said twice, once by each path. A new announced definition row therefore needs no change to the table: the set is computed from the variables.
- `Bbs757LightTableTests` pins it (`Watch_set_skips_names_announced_elsewhere`, `Momentary_buttons_are_not_watched`, `A_light_and_a_switch_sharing_a_var_are_watched_once`, `The_real_watch_set_leaves_out_the_definitions_announced_rows`). Plan 13, 2026-10-27 and 28; never timed: one watch read in the app's own log. → [b757.md](../b757.md)

## B757-50

- The 757's MCP Mach is read only from the speed window's wheels, `Bbs757Mcp.MachFromDrums` over `L:B757_MCP_Gau_SPD_AUTO_100` and `L:B757_MCP_Gau_SPD_AUTO_10`. Never from `L:BBS_TARGET_MACH`. A reading below .40 is no Mach, and neither is a wheel caught between two digits or an unread one: `MachFromDrums` answers null.
- Why. Measured in flight on 2026-10-26, level at FL290, 282 kt and Mach 0.73, the window opened by Speed Intervene and changed to Mach with the IAS/MACH button (`L:BBS_AIRSPEED_IN_MACH` 1): `L:BBS_TARGET_MACH` stayed 0, so it does not follow the target. The speed bug variable became the target's IAS equivalent (281.1, fractional). The wheels showed the Mach. They are wheel positions, not digits: the hundreds wheel reads 10 × its digit, the tens and units wheels 10 × (digit + 1), so 0 reads 10 and 8 reads 90 (in IAS at 283 kt they read 20, 90, 40). In Mach the hundreds wheel is the tenths digit and the tens wheel the hundredths: 70 and 40 are ".73"; the units wheel sits on a constant and is not used.
- Why below .40 is knots. The wheels show knots in IAS, and for 1 to 2 s after a changeover they still do: the wheels move 1 to 2 s after the call that changes them. A speed of 100 to 399 kt decodes as .10 to .39, and BlueBird's Mach target stops at a floor of .40 (measured: a burst that would have reached .39 stopped at .40), so anything under .40 is the wheels still showing knots and is never spoken or used as a Mach. A Mach set waits for the wheels to show a Mach that holds before it sends anything ("Mach unavailable" if none comes within `Bbs757Mach.WheelWaitMs`).
- `Bbs757McpTests` and `Bbs757MachTests` pin it (`Mach_from_the_window_wheels`, `Wheels_that_are_not_a_mach_read_as_unread`, `Wheels_that_never_show_a_mach_send_nothing`). → [b757.md](../b757.md)

## B757-51

- A Mach set never commands past .86, the aircraft's limit (`Bbs757Mach.Max`; the entry takes 0.40 to 0.86, `Bbs757Mach.Min` and `Max`). `Bbs757Mach.Bursts` plans the set's one tiered burst with no event past the 25th, and `Bbs757Mach.SetMachAsync` corrects only in linear bursts of at most `Bbs757Mcp.LinearBurstMax` (10) events and never sends more in the set's direction, in all, than the difference it planned at the start; an overshoot is brought back.
- Why. BlueBird does not stop the Mach target at .86. Measured in flight on 2026-10-26 at FL290 with `K:AP_SPD_VAR_INC` and `DEC`, one calculator call a burst: one event moves the target 0.01; bursts of 1, 5 and 10 moved it 1, 5 and 10 hundredths; a burst of 20 down from .69 stopped at .40 (the tiers of [B757-3] would give 30 hundredths, a linear burst 20); and a burst of 30 up from .40 reached .95. So the floor is .40, there is no ceiling at .86, and a burst speeds up as the knots do (events 1 to 10 one hundredth each, 11 to 25 two each). What events 26 to 30 do is not known: that one run cannot tell three hundredths an event from a ceiling at .95, which is why no burst is planned past 25 events.
- Why the corrections are capped. The wheels the set is judged by ([B757-50]) lag the events by 1 to 2 s. A burst that seems to have fallen short may still be rolling in, and a correction sent on top of it would carry the target past what the pilot typed; near .86 that is past the aircraft's limit, into a speed BlueBird will fly. So the set waits for the wheels to move and then for two agreeing reads, and its corrections toward the target can never add up to more than the planned difference.
- `Bbs757MachTests` runs the set against a simulated MCP with BlueBird's tiers, up and down (`Mach_set_against_a_fake_mcp_up`, `Mach_set_against_a_fake_mcp_down`). → [b757.md](../b757.md)

## B757-52

- A row whose call-out depends on another row delivered in the same batch is judged once the whole batch is in, never as each value arrives. The MCP speed and Mach rows are the case: the definition's `ProcessAutoflightUpdate` only stores the speed, the two Mach wheels, the Mach flag (`L:BBS_AIRSPEED_IN_MACH`) and the blank flag (`L:BBS_IAS_BLANKED`) and marks the rows dirty; `EvaluateSpeedRows` judges them together on the rest timer's tick. A batch is delivered in one go on the UI thread, so a tick never falls inside one.
- Why. The app delivers a batch's values sorted by name, so the speed arrives before the Mach flag and the blank flag, and the Mach's hundredths wheel before its tenths wheel. Judged as each arrives, a row is judged against the other rows' old values: at a changeover from IAS to Mach the speed variable turns into the Mach's IAS equivalent and arrives while the flag still reads IAS, so it would be taken for a speed the pilot set; when VNAV blanks the window, its target arrives before the blank flag does; and a Mach going from .79 to .80 would be judged with the new hundredths and the old tenths, as .70. The review of plan 05 found it (its findings 2 and 3) and it was fixed the day the plan landed, 2026-10-26.
- What the judging does with the whole set: a change of the blank flag or the Mach flag is a changeover, VNAV taking or giving back the speed, or Speed Intervene, so both rows take their new values as already spoken and anything waiting to be said is dropped; otherwise a changed speed is spoken only while the window is open in IAS, a changed Mach only while it is open in Mach ([B757-50] for what counts as a Mach). A new row that reads two variables of one batch follows the same shape: store, mark dirty, judge on the tick.
- `Bbs757AutoflightAnnouncementsTests` pins it (`Mach_only_in_mach_mode`, `Mach_to_ias_in_one_batch_says_nothing`). → [b757.md](../b757.md)

## B757-53

- A state of a `Bbs757Annunciators` row that the pilot need not hear is a phrase that starts with `Bbs757Annunciators.Quiet`, never null. A null phrase means the value cannot be read as a state at all (an unreadable value, a door between its ends) and is not tracked. `Bbs757Annunciators.IsSpoken` is what keeps a quiet phrase from being said.
- Why. `Bbs757ChangeTracker.Observe` speaks a change from a remembered phrase, and takes a row's first phrase for a silent baseline (the cockpit as found is no change). If a row's unspoken state were null, the tracker would remember nothing for it, and the first spoken state after it would be that baseline: an altitude alert coming on after the light had been out, "IRS L aligned" after the align light had been flashing, a start valve closing after it had been open, would each be the one change the row exists to say, and each would be lost. With a quiet phrase the tracker holds "quiet: altitude alert out", and the change to "Altitude alert" is heard. Found while building the rows (plan 06, 2026-10-26 and 27); the rows that use it are the altitude alert and the configuration warning going out, an IRS aligning or its align light out, a start valve open, and a blank autoland status.
- A related must, which a test already pins: a linked row's read-back and its own phrase are the same words. A press from the app reads its status row back and says it ("EXT PWR on"), then rebaselines the row with `BBS757Definition.LinkedPhrase`; if the row's own phrase for that state were other words ("External power on"), the delivery after the press would differ from the baseline and announce the press a second time. `Bbs757AnnunciatorsTests.Ext_pwr_press_reads_back` and `Ext_pwr_change_announced_once` fail on it.
- `Bbs757AnnunciatorsTests` pins the quiet states (`Alerts_speak_when_they_light`, `Irs_aligned_only_in_nav`, `Start_valve_closed_is_said_at_cutout_and_open_is_quiet`). The same reasoning is why a flight load keeps the tracker's baselines (`Bbs757ChangeTracker.BeginLoadQuiet`: changes are tracked and unspoken for a while, the baselines stay): the batch delivers a value only when it changes, so a baseline thrown away at a load would make a row's first real change a silent baseline again. → [b757.md](../b757.md)

## B757-54

- The 757 never registers a second Continuous row on the stock `TRANSPONDER CODE:1`. The squawk watch, which speaks a squawk change from any source ("Squawk 4743"), is the transponder panel's own read-back row: `Bbs757Annunciators.SquawkWatchKey` is `Bbs757Squawk.CodeKey` (`BBS757_SQUAWK`), made continuous and announced so that it rides the 1 Hz batch, and still shown on the panel as a read-only status row.
- Why. Plan 14 (2026-10-26 and 27) first added a watch row of its own, `BBS757_SQUAWK_WATCH`, on the same variable. Two continuous, batched rows of one name break the batch: it is sorted by name, and a duplicate shifts every later slot of the struct, so every variable after it reads another's value ([VAR-7]). `VarNameCollisionTests` failed on it, and the watch became the existing row. The two keys are one on purpose; a change that splits them again, or adds any other continuous row on that variable, brings the shift back.
- The app's own squawk set is not repeated by the watch: the set arms the row's echo (`BBS757Definition.ArmOwnSquawkEcho`). `Bbs757AnnunciatorsTests` pins both (`Squawk_change_from_elsewhere_is_spoken`, `Own_squawk_set_not_repeated`, `Watches_registered_as_announced_rows_with_ctrl_m_rows`). → [b757.md](../b757.md)
