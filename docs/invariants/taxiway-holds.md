# Taxiway hold placement: rules in full

Each section is the complete text of one rule. Its one-line form, under the same ID, is in `.claude/rules/taxiway-holds.md`, which Claude Code loads when it reads matching code. Background: [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md).

## TWH-1

- **A hold short of a taxiway stops before the taxiway, set back so the whole aircraft stays clear of it.** Both kinds go through one placement, `TaxiwayHoldPlacement.Choose`, called from `TaxiGuidanceManager.PlaceTaxiwayHoldStop`: a row's **Hold short of runway** list set to a taxiway ("via A, hold short of taxiway B", `ApplyUserTaxiwayHoldShorts`) and a Progressive Taxi leg that ends "hold short of taxiway B" (`ApplyLegEndSetback`). Until stage 1 of the Taxi UI Redesign both stopped AT the junction node, which puts the nose on the taxiway; at KCLE 2026-11-14 ("P, hold short of taxiway L") the pilot stopped 21.0 m short of the junction without being told to. The setback is `SetbackMetres(wingspan)`: `JunctionHalfWidthM` (15 m, half a wide taxiway plus a margin) plus `NoseShareOfWingspan` (0.55) of the wingspan, `DefaultWingspanM` (36 m) when it is unknown, clamped to `MinSetbackM` and `MaxSetbackM` (25 to 60 m): 34.8 m for a 36 m wingspan. The form sets `AircraftWingspanMetres` before each Calculate.

  The stop, in order:
  1. An existing route node at least the setback before the junction (less `NodeMatchToleranceM`, 1 m) and at most `ExistingNodeReachM` (40 m) beyond it.
  2. On a pilot's Calculate only, a node inserted on the route's own edge exactly the setback back (`TaxiGraph.InsertHoldingPointNodeOnEdge`, kept only when the edge it split is that segment's own; otherwise the junction, logged). Never within `MinFromRouteStartM` (10 m) of the route's start, and never on an off-chart or uncharted segment (TWH-2).
  3. Only without inserting (a recalculation, or the setback point on an off-chart or uncharted leg), an existing node up to `RecalcMaxBackM` (150 m) back. A recalculation never inserts a node ([RTE-30]); the 1 m tolerance lets it find the node inserted at load, measured from the split edges.
  4. Otherwise the junction itself: a route shorter than the setback, no node in reach, a setback point within 10 m of the route start, or an insert that failed.

  **Runways.** A candidate (a node or an inserted point) is usable only when the whole route from `ProgressiveLegEnd.CaptureRadiusM` (25 m) BEFORE it up to the junction is clear of every runway's margin (`RouteRunwayCrossings.RunwayWithinClearMargin`, the margin the automatic runway pass demands of a stop it invents), sampled at most `SampleStepM` (5 m) apart along each segment, not only at its nodes. Where that stretch runs off the start of the route, only the part that exists is judged. Why the 25 m: a mid-route hold is captured `WAYPOINT_CAPTURE_RADIUS_M` (25 m, the same value, `LegEndWiringTests` pins it) before its stop, and a leg end at `CaptureRadiusM`; the plan's first rule checked only the stretch from the stop on, so a stop moved forward to a node 5 m past a runway's margin was captured, and a leg ended, with the aircraft still on the pavement, with "Slow down" and "Stop." spoken there (whole-stage review, Critical 1; spec 8.5, 8.8). VirtualPilot's `legends` found two such legs, "Slow down" said on the pavement (RKSI "via C9, hold short of B3", KLSV "via A, hold short of F"), and none after the fix. Sampling, not nodes alone: a runway crossed inside one edge has no node on it, and each of rules 1 to 3 was caught in review landing behind such a runway (`Rule_1_never_lands_behind_a_runway_crossed_inside_one_edge` and its two siblings). A refused candidate moves forward, to the first node whose 25 m approach is clear, else to the junction; it never moves back. A junction inside a margin, or whose own 25 m approach is not clear, gets no stop (no point before it can be clear either): a row pick says so in the summary ("Note: no place to hold short of taxiway B clear of the runway; no stop will be called there.") and the runway's own automatic stop still stands before that runway; a leg end keeps the junction as built, and never ends on runway pavement (STR-25). Such a junction or first node past a runway can leave the nose of a large aircraft on the taxiway: the stop is close to it by necessity (KLSV "via A, hold short of F": the stop is the F junction, 03L lies just before it, and the aircraft stood 18.4 m from it).

  **The leg end.** On a pilot's Calculate a "hold short of taxiway B" leg is first cut to the FIRST meeting with B along the route from the last row's taxiway that is clear of every runway's margin, which a Turn pick on an earlier row can have moved, then set back. A meeting inside a margin is skipped, not refused: the route then crosses B before the leg holds short of a later meeting. The owner's ruling of 2026-11-25 ("Warn at Calculate") kept the skip and made it heard: when the skipped meeting is the first along the last row's run, Calculate says, warnings first, "Taxiway B meets taxiway A first beside runway 27; you will cross B there and hold short of it further on." (`ClearanceRouteChecks.RunwayBesideFirstMeeting` on the route as cut, `MetFirstBesideARunway`, the runway named by `RouteRunwayCrossings.RunwayNameWithinClearMargin`; `ClearanceRouteChecksTests`, `ClearanceWiringTests`). Until then nothing was said, and the stage 1 review had found a rare leg driving through B before holding short of it, silently. A row pick, by contrast, is judged at its first meeting only. The junction stays `_destinationNodeId`, so a recalculation that routes afresh reaches it and is cut back again, without inserting (TWH-2 for a kept tail). Where the form finds the junction: STR-25.

  **The tick-box stop keeps its place.** The row's **Hold short** tick box ("Hold short of end of taxiway A", `ApplyUserHoldShorts`) is not this placement: it stays at the end of the row's taxiway, where it meets the next one, with its own label (HLD-2).

  **Unconfirmed.** `JunctionHalfWidthM`, `NoseShareOfWingspan`, `DefaultWingspanM`, the two clamps and `ExistingNodeReachM` are assumptions the owner has not yet confirmed in the sim. The only measurement so far is VirtualPilot's `legends` mode (2026-11-24, 40 airports, fs2024 navdata, a 36 m wingspan; docs/virtual-pilot.md, "Leg ends and taxiway holds"; figures after the review fixes): the stop at load lay a median 37.3 m before the junction without Turn picks (0 to 72.8 m: KLSV's is the junction, every other 33.9 m or more) and 40.8 m with them; the aircraft stood a median 54.5 m before the junction without picks (min 18.4, p90 69.5) and 60.1 m with them (min 32.4, p90 81.0), never at or past it; 97 legs stood more than 60 m back (an existing node up to 40 m beyond the setback, then the 25 m capture). The figures with picks are the 2026-11-25 re-run, after the owner's "Stop before the turn" ruling (TWH-2); they were 35.5 m, and 56.5 m (min 16.4, p90 75.5), with 83 legs over 60 m. Re-measure with it, and ask the owner, before changing a constant. → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md), [virtual-pilot.md](../virtual-pilot.md)

## TWH-2

- **A taxiway stop is placed on the route the Turn picks leave, as if they were not set.** In `LoadRoute`, `ApplyLegEndSetback` runs after `TurnPickApplier.Apply` and before the pilot's own hold-short picks, and `ApplyUserTaxiwayHoldShorts` runs among those picks (`LegEndWiringTests`): a pick can replace the route and move the junction (TURN-1), so a stop placed first would be on a route then thrown away. On a recalculation the setback runs before the sanity gate, so the gate compares two cut routes. Each clause exists because its absence broke something:
  - **It never splits an off-chart leg.** An off-chart or uncharted segment (`IsOffChartTurn`, `IsUncharted`) has no graph edge to insert a node on. `Choose` refuses rule 2 there and takes an existing node up to 150 m back, or the junction (`An_off_chart_leg_is_never_split`).
  - **A picked turn does not hold the stop on its taxiway.** `PlaceTaxiwayHoldStop` places every taxiway stop with `Choose` and no floor, as for a row with no pick: B less than the setback past the turn is held short of BEFORE the turn, on the taxiway before it, the whole aircraft clear of B; B further on is held short of after the turn, as before. The owner's ruling of 2026-11-25 ("Stop before the turn"): until then the pick's `FirstTaxiwaySegment` floored the stop (`firstUsableIndex`, which `Choose` keeps, with its tests), so "Via F" stopped on Z before the turn while "Left onto F", over the same route, stopped just after it with the nose able to be on B (`A_turn_too_close_to_the_taxiway_keeps_the_junction`): VirtualPilot's `legends` had eight picked legs standing 16 to 23 m from the junction.
  - **A leg ends before a picked turn's link where it can.** A stop that ENDS a leg goes through `TaxiwayHoldPlacement.PlaceLegEnd` (`ChooseForLegEnd` gives its stop) with every pick on the route, any row's: where `Choose` would put it on, or at the far end of, a pick's link from its departure node to its join (an off-chart leg or a connector, `IsOnALink`: a node, a point inserted on it, or the join), the leg ends where that turn starts, at its departure node (the end of segment `FirstSegmentAfterDeparture - 1`) when `Choose` would take that node (the route from the capture radius before it to the junction clear of every runway), else the next such node before it on no link (`LegEndStop.AtTurnStart`; logged "the leg ends where that turn starts"). Nothing is inserted there, and the pick is then dropped like any pick a leg ends before. When no node before the turn is usable, `Choose`'s own stop stands, on the link, and the pick with it (logged "no node before that turn is usable; the stop stays there, and the pick with it"): a first-row turn that departs from the route's own start (`FirstSegmentAfterDeparture` 0: an already-made pick whose walk back reaches a stand's unnamed lead-in, or a route-changing pick with nothing arriving at the turn) has no node before its link, and a runway margin between 25 m before the departure node and 25 m before the stop blocks every node before it. The leg then ends on the link, the route making the turn: its notes and an off-chart leg's sentence are said, and `ApplyLegEndSetback` drops only a pick whose turn starts past the leg's last segment (`FirstSegmentAfterDeparture`). The first version of this rule gave no stop there, and a leg end with no stop keeps the junction itself: the nose on B, nothing said, where `Choose` had a stop the setback back with a clear approach (re-review, fix round 2). Only where `Choose` itself finds no stop is there none (`NoSafeStop`, the leg end keeping the junction as for any junction with no usable approach). Tests: `A_first_row_turn_from_the_route_start_keeps_the_ordinary_stop`, `A_runway_before_the_turn_start_keeps_the_ordinary_stop`, `A_leg_end_has_no_stop_only_where_Choose_has_none`.
  - **A recalculation keeps a turn-start end.** At load, `ApplyLegEndSetback` records an end placed at a turn's start (`_legEndAtTurnStart`: the stop node and the pick's row; cleared by `LoadRoute` and `StopGuidance`, in its Fail snapshot and its rollback, [RTE-29]). `TryRecalculateRoute` then routes to that stop node (`recalcDestinationNodeId`, also for its near-destination guard, component, reachability, bearing and reached tests), over only the rows still ahead that lead to it (`TaxiwayHoldPlacement.RowsLeadingToTurnStart`; none left: the shortest way there), and never sets it back again. Routed afresh to the junction instead, with the pick gone, the route could take the charted way and `Choose` put the stop near B after the turn (ZSPD's end, 128 m before C at load, near C after an off-route recalculation; re-review, fix round 2). `LegEndWiringTests.A_leg_end_at_a_turn_start_is_kept_by_a_recalculation`, `A_recalculation_to_a_turn_start_follows_only_the_rows_before_the_turn`. Without this a leg ended on the off-chart leg itself, the pick dropped and its "Off-chart leg" sentence never said (`legends` with plain `Choose`: ZSPD "C2, P2, left onto P2, hold short of C", `LEGEND_ENDS_OFFCHART`). An interim fix re-placed such a stop with the old floor, on the picked taxiway; the stage-3 review found it brought back what the owner ruled out: ZSPD's point 1.1 m before a P2 node found no edge to split, so the stop fell back to the junction and the aircraft stood 19.0 m from C after its picked turn; and with B between the setback less the 1 m node tolerance and the setback past the join, the floored rule 2 landed on the link below the floor and the forward move took a node closer to B, or the junction. The coordinator's ruling (2026-11-25, following the owner's): the turn's departure node, for the leg end only. Tests: `A_leg_end_at_the_far_end_of_an_off_chart_leg_stops_where_the_turn_starts` (the ZSPD shape), `A_join_within_the_node_tolerance_of_the_setback_also_stops_where_the_turn_starts`, `A_leg_end_at_an_earlier_rows_off_chart_join_stops_where_that_turn_starts`, `A_leg_end_on_a_connector_link_stops_where_the_turn_starts`. A mid-route stop is placed by plain `Choose`, with no exception: the route carries on after Continue, so the turn is made and its off-chart leg announced, and a stop at an off-chart leg's join is a stop like any other (`A_mid_route_stop_at_an_off_chart_join_stays_where_Choose_puts_it`).
  - **A leg end before the turn drops the pick, unannounced.** A Turn pick whose turn starts past a leg end's stop (`FirstSegmentAfterDeparture` past its last segment) is dropped from the route and logged, and `LoadRoute` composes the Turn pick note from the picks still on the route (`route.TurnPicks`, never `TurnPickResult.Applied`): "Turning left onto taxiway F, as picked." or an off-chart note for a turn the pilot never makes would be false (`legends`' `LEGEND_DROPPED_PICK_SPOKEN`: none). A mid-route stop before the turn keeps its pick: the turn is made after Continue, and a split before it moves its indices up (below).
  - **Every split shifts the indices.** A split turns segment m into two, so every Turn pick's `FirstSegmentAfterDeparture` and `FirstTaxiwaySegment` past m moves up by one (`ShiftPicksAfterSplit`), and so does every deferred runway pick's `RunStart` (retried after adoption, HLD-16). Left unshifted, the off-chart sentence, the missed-turn call and a kept-tail recalculation all read the wrong segment. A split also moves every runway event already built up by one past m, its `StopNodeIndex` and `ReachNodeIndex` (`ShiftEventsAfterSplit`, on the honoured runway picks' events and the route's own), and `Split` keeps a stop's placed label and cleared runways on the far half, where the stop stays: left unshifted, a cleared crossing is matched, and its stop cleared, at the wrong node ([CLR-2], `A_split_keeps_an_events_stop_on_the_same_node`). The one-line rule does not name it, having no room; CLR-2's full text, whose rule line is in `.claude/rules/cleared-crossings.md`, does.
  - **A kept tail is never cut again.** A recalculation that keeps a Turn pick's loaded turn and tail (`TurnPickCarryOver.TailFrom`, TURN-3) ends at the stop placed at load, and `TryRecalculateRoute` skips `ApplyLegEndSetback` for it (`keepsLoadedTail`), as it does for a turn-start end (above). The end-node test alone cut such a tail again whenever its stop was the junction itself, and the second cut moved the stop back again.

  VirtualPilot `legends` (2026-11-24): 42 picks changed the route; no `LEGEND_PICK_INDEX`, `LEGEND_OFFCHART_SPLIT`, `LEGEND_ENDS_OFFCHART` or `LEGEND_PICK_NOT_FIRST_JUNCTION` finding, and with a pick applied the stop never moved back before the turn; without one, 46 of the 56 refused picks' routes ended on the taxiway before the turn (KLSV "E, F").
